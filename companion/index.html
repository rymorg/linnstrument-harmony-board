Harmony Board

The left six columns become a harmony zone: key, mode, alterations, chord focus and memories. The other 19 columns play in the current scale with full MPE. In Perform mode, five of those columns become left-hand chords that latch and keep time. Press or drag on the playing pads to hear them: sideways is pitch, up and down is timbre. Write a song

Demo song: First Light

A verse in A minor, a chorus in C, a Lydian lift and a last chorus in D. About 50 seconds at 92 BPM, played on the board above using only the steps below.

Press play and watch the board. The demo taps the harmony pads and plays the surface the way you would. White pads are sounding.

  1. Pick a home and a mood. Tap a Key pad, then a Mode pad. Higher Mode pads sound brighter: Lydian is dreamy, Ionian happy, Mixolydian bluesy, Dorian soulful, Aeolian sad, Phrygian dark. Demo: A Aeolian.
  2. Learn one chord shape. With rows a 4th apart, a 7th chord is a diamond: the root, the 3rd up and to the left, the 5th up and to the right, and the 7th two rows above the root. Leave off the top pad for a triad. The shape never changes; the mode decides whether it comes out major or minor.
  3. Loop four chords. Tap a Chord pad to light that chord, then put the diamond on a cyan pad. Start with a loop you already know. Demo: i – ♭VI – ♭III – ♭VII, which is Am7, Fmaj7, Cmaj7, G7.
  4. Sing over it. Land on lit pads on the strong beats and use unlit pads to get between them. Every pad is in the scale, so no note is wrong, only more or less tense. Slide into notes for a vocal feel. Demo: bar 4 slides from F down to D.
  5. Add pull with one alteration. Flip degree 7 to ♮7. The V chord becomes E7, which leans hard back to the home chord. Tap RST once it resolves. Demo: bars 5–6.
  6. Change sections while holding. Keep the chord down and tap REL: Am7 glides into Cmaj7 and you are in the relative major. For the chorus, switch to triads, push the rhythm and sing higher. Demo: I – V – vi – IV.
  7. Lift with a brighter mode. Tap Lydian. F becomes F♯ and the II chord turns major. Two bars of I – II are enough to lift into the last chorus.
  8. End higher. Tap Ionian, then Key D. The chorus moves up a whole step with the same fingering. Finish on a 9th chord and push your fingers up to open the sound.
  9. Save and replay. The demo stores each section in Memory 5–8. Recall them with the Memory pads, or with footswitches assigned to PREV and NEXT (the ← → keys here), and play your own melody over them.

What makes it sound good

  • Chord tones on the beat. Lit pads on strong beats sound settled; unlit pads in between add motion.
  • Small moves between chords. Am7 and Fmaj7 share three notes, so the change sounds smooth.
  • One change at a time. Change the chord, the mode, one note or the key, but not all at once.
  • Contrast between sections. Low and sustained in the verse, higher with rhythm in the chorus, and a lift before the last chorus.

Demo song: Night Drive

Three sections stored in Memory, then played in Perform mode: latched chords in the left hand, melody in the right, and the footswitch for each change. About 55 seconds at 100 BPM.

Press play. The demo sets up three sections in Compose mode, switches to Perform mode, then plays with both hands.

  1. Set up sections in Compose mode. Pick a key and mode, choose a chord pattern on the Harmony page (hold Split), and store it in Memory. Demo: Memory 5 is D Dorian with Pulse, 6 is B♭ Lydian with Strum, 7 is D Dorian with Arpeggio.
  2. Switch to Perform mode. Tap Split, the Harmony button. Columns 1–5 become chord pads: rows are I to VII, and the columns are Root, 1st inversion, 2nd inversion, 7th and Smart. Memory and the melody pads don’t move.
  3. Latch a chord. Tap Latch in the thumb strip (the bottom row), then a chord. It keeps playing in the section’s pattern, so your left hand is free. Tap another chord to switch, or the same one to stop.
  4. Let Smart voice it. The Smart column picks the voicing nearest the last chord, so changes move as few notes as possible. Demo: Dm7 to G7 moves two notes, one step each.
  5. Play the melody. The lit pads on the right follow the left-hand chord, so you can always see its tones.
  6. Walk the bass with inversions. The inversion columns put the 3rd or 5th in the bass. Demo: G/B leads down a half step to B♭.
  7. Change sections with your foot. Each footswitch press recalls the next memory, so key, mode and pattern change together. Tap the new chord right on the change.
  8. Add color from the thumb strip. Tap V/x, then a chord, to play the dominant 7th that leads into it. Demo: V/x + i = A7, which pulls back to Dm.
  9. End it. On the Harmony page, set Pattern to Sustain, then tap add9 and i for a held Dm9. Drag up on the chord pad to open the sound.

Why Perform mode is easier to play

  • One job per limb. The left hand picks chords, the right hand plays melody, and your foot changes sections.
  • Latch frees your hand. A latched chord keeps time on its own while you reach for a modifier or play melody with both hands.
  • Smart voicing does the arranging. Chords move to the nearest notes, which is what makes changes sound smooth.
  • Nothing moves under you. Switching modes or sections never moves the melody pads; only the lights change.

How the board splits

In the scale-step layout each column is the next note of the current scale, so the 19 playing columns span about 2.7 octaves per row. The stock 25-column chromatic layout spans just over two, so you lose no range per row. Rows sit a 4th apart in scale steps. Any chord shape then plays the matching chord of whatever mode is active: the same three fingers give a minor triad in Dorian and a diminished one on the 7th degree of Ionian.

Perform mode

Tapping Split (the Harmony button) swaps columns 1–5 between the harmony controls and a left-hand chord split. Memory and the melody pads never move, so switching mid-song is safe.

ColumnIn Perform mode
0Tapping Split (the Harmony button) switches Compose and Perform. The chord pattern (Sustain, Pulse, Strum or Arpeggio, following the MIDI clock) is set on the Harmony page.
1–5Chords. Rows 2–8 are I to VII. The columns are root position, 1st inversion, 2nd inversion, 7th chord and Smart (the voicing nearest the last chord). The inversion columns put that note in the bass. Row 1 is a thumb strip: V/x, Borrow, sus4, add9 and Latch.
6Memory. Each slot also stores the chord pattern, so the footswitch changes key, mode and groove together.
7–25Melody. The lit pads follow whatever chord the left hand is playing.

In the firmware, the chord and its bass note go out together on MIDI channel 16, and the melody keeps MPE on channel 1 with member channels 2–8. Put a second instrument on channel 16 for the left hand. The chord pad’s Y goes out as CC74 and its pressure as channel pressure, both on channel 16. Because the chord notes share a channel, they can’t bend one by one, so a sounding chord re-strikes when a harmony change moves its notes (Held notes set to Hold leaves it ringing). One touch starts up to six notes. Patterns will reuse the arpeggiator’s timing and the MIDI clock, so they follow your DAW’s tempo.

Color guidance

Color shows where you could go next, not just where you are. The LED driver gains a half-brightness mode and three new blends (teal, azure, violet), made the same way as orange and lime by alternating colors on each refresh, so they don't flicker.

  • Surface: Guide lights mark chord tones, color tones and notes to pass through. Degrees gives each scale degree its own hue; Notes gives each note name its own hue around the circle of fifths. Notes that a harmony change brings in pulse for a moment.
  • Chord pads: colored by function (home, away, tension), bright for a natural next move and pulsing when they resolve the current tension.
  • Key, Mode and Memory pads: colored by how many notes would change, so smooth and dramatic moves are visible before you make them.
  • Styles: Pop, Rock, Blues, Jazz, Soul, Cinematic, Flamenco and Free swap the rules behind the same colors: which moves count as natural, where the music wants to resolve, which notes are flavor rather than clashes, and in Blues and Flamenco the chord qualities themselves (dominant 7ths on I, IV and V; a major I with a ♭9). Each style has progression templates whose next chord pulses. Memory stores the style, so one song can switch styles between sections.
  • Harmony page: hold Split and the playing surface becomes a page, the same way LinnStrument's own settings screens work. One column per setting, read from the top pad down: Harmony on/off, Compose/Perform, Style, Template, Lights, Hints, Layout, Rows apart, Held notes and Pattern. Tap Split to go back to playing.

Control buttons and footswitches

Harmony gets exactly one button. Split becomes the Harmony button (splits and the harmony zone both want the left side of the board), and every other control button keeps its factory job, including Per-Split Settings, which is where MPE is set up. Footswitches stay on their factory defaults (Arpeggiator on the left, Sustain on the right). To step memories with your feet, assign them to PREV and NEXT in Global Settings: those are the sequencer's existing assignments, which have nothing else to do in Harmony mode, so the stored settings stay safe if you go back to the stock firmware. Presets will also save the Harmony setup.

Cutting features mainly buys simpler code and free RAM. Each cell read waits on analog settling delays in ls_sensor, so per-note latency improves only a little. The lookup table also makes note lookup cheaper than the current per-touch calculation.

Decisions for you

  1. Default layout: scale steps (this mockup's default) or chromatic with scale lights?
  2. What held notes do when the harmony changes: Morph, Hold or Retrigger?
  3. Six zone columns, or five without Memory to get 20 playing columns?
  4. Should harmony changes also go out over MIDI (NRPN or SysEx) so a DAW or plugin can follow along?
  5. Should Memory also store the left-hand chord, so the footswitch can step through a whole progression?
